## 2026-11-13 · [REG] REG-21 (K3, step B): does the stuck server do the work of the all-miss server? Its uncached-token work rate against the saturated cache-off sweep's, at each load; batch 1 on the 13 closed-loop burst runs made before REG-19, batch 2 on REG-19's burst runs after its [RESULT] (0 GPU h)

- **ID:** REG-21. An analysis of existing runs, 0 GPU h. Draft 4. Drafts 1 and 2 are in `docs/drafts/scratchpad-2026-11-12/`, draft 3 in `docs/drafts/reg21_draft3.md` with its rule F review in `docs/drafts/reviews-2026-11-13/rulef-reg21-v3-2.md` (NOT READY, nine required changes; what was changed because of it is listed at the end).
- **How this registration came about.** Draft 1 judged model F's long-run mix values on 900 s windows; its review showed that such a window is not in the long run. Draft 2 replayed model F on each run's own schedule and judged the capacities at which the replay reproduces the run's served-turn mix and throughput; its round-2 review found that the time a request stays in the running batch moves the mix and so confounds both, and that the E1 item could change no decision. The owner's choices for step B were "Register now, mix primary", then "Rebuild around per-run sim" (decisions.md 2026-11-13 [CORR]). **This registration departs from "mix primary":** the mix is set by queue kinematics and the residence in the batch, not by what K3 asks. The mix and the replay stay as reported readouts. The departure is the delegate's and is listed for the owner.
- **Question:** in the cold, backlogged state after a burst, does the engine prefill uncached prompt tokens at the rate of the saturated engine with caching off (J1), and is that rate the same at λ = 2.0 and 2.4 (J2)?
- **Claim tested:** K3 (`docs/claims.md`). It is the one statement all three preprints make of their own engines, tested here on another engine and workload (BELIEF as to the papers; anchors in `agent/LITERATURE.md`): Valse and Jannesari's registered trap rule asks for an "uncached prefill token rate within ±15% of the all-miss rate" (their Table 1) and their model's premise (F1) is that every turn misses; Huang and Liu report that turn throughput in the degraded regime "is that of the same engine with prefix caching disabled" (their §3); Lu et al. report uncached prefill "near the saturated" prefill throughput in their trapped run (their §3). Valse and Jannesari's own token-rate ratios over 57 stuck runs were 0.95 to 1.12 (their §4).

### Statistic (`analysis/k3w.py`)
- **U** (`work_rate`): uncached prompt tokens per second, Σ (prompt_tokens − cached_tokens) over ok requests, session and burst, whose first token falls in the window (the first token ends a request's prefill). It raises if an ok request has no first-token time or no prompt length.
- **U_off** (`reference`): the mean of U over REG-8's five saturated cache-off runs, pinned by id (`k3w.REG21["ref_runs"]`: `sweep_l1pl350_pc_off` at λ = 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, the runs that give the floor 1.681 req/s; decisions.md 2026-11-07 [RESULT] REG-8), each over its measured window. It raises unless exactly those five are there and valid. All five count, whatever their own gauges say; r against the four runs at λ ≥ 2.5 is reported beside r.
- **r** = U over W ÷ U_off, per run. W = [burst end + 600 s, burst end + watch): [1680, 2580) s; [1680, 5580) s for REG-15; [1680, 4080) s for REG-19's runs.
- **Why r:** it contains no turn mix, no residence, no queue discipline and no think time. On synthetic servers (`tests/test_k3w.py`; no run read): an all-miss first-come-first-served server reads r = 1.00 ± 0.02 at λ = 2.0 and 2.4; so does a running batch of 16 or of 64 slots (the latter moves the mean served turn index by more than 0.03, draft 2's confound) and a six-fold think time; a fixed cost per turn moves r by under 0.02; a trap at 1.85 or 1.55 req/s reads 1.10 or 0.92; a work rate that differs by load fails J2.
- **What r cannot see:** hits below the precondition (r subtracts cached tokens; the precondition, not J1, answers whether the state is all-miss), and prefill done twice after a preemption (each request's tokens are counted once).

### Runs and preconditions
- **Batch 1** (`k3w.REG21["batch1"]`): the 13 valid closed-loop L1 burst runs at 350 W made before REG-19: REG-11 (λ = 2.4, seed 1), REG-12 (2.4, seeds 2–6), REG-14 (2.2, seed 2), REG-15 (2.2, seed 12), REG-17 (2.0, seed 2), REG-18 (2.0, seeds 3–6).
- **Batch 2** (`k3w.batch2_runs`): REG-19's burst runs, one valid run per seed, the seeds a prefix of 15–19. The function raises until `docs/decisions.md` has REG-19's [RESULT] heading, on two valid runs of a seed and on a gap.
- **A run enters** when `k1.cold_backlogged` holds in its own end window. **It is judged** only if, in W, it is (a) all-miss: prefix reuse of later turns (`k1.reuse`) ≤ 0.05; and (b) **busy: at least one request waiting in at least 0.99 of the scrapes** (`k3w.busy_share`). An engine whose line empties in part of W does less work for a reason that is not its capacity; the review's synthetic trace of an all-miss server with a lull reads r = 0.69 to 0.91. Runs left out are listed with the reason ("not all-miss in W", "idle in W", "no later turn in W").

### Judged (`k3w.judge`; thresholds fixed here, before U or r is computed on any run)
| # | Statement | inside if | undefined if |
|---|---|---|---|
| J2, load (batch 1) | one work rate at λ = 2.0 and 2.4 | the medians of r of the load groups with at least 3 judged runs differ by at most 0.05 | fewer than 2 such groups |
| J1, level | the stuck engine works at the cache-off token rate | \|median of r − 1\| ≤ 0.05 | fewer than 3 judged runs |

- **Pooling (rule H).** In batch 1 the median of J1 pools the loads only if J2 is inside: J2's statement is the assumption that makes them one population. If J2 is outside or undefined, J1 is given per load group and the pooled label is "not_pooled". The two runs at λ = 2.2 (REG-14, REG-15, with windows of 900 s and 3900 s) are reported and enter a pooled median; as a group of two they do not enter J2.
- **Batch 2** has one load: J1 only. The batches are judged apart and never pooled. **If they disagree, both are reported, batch 2 governs what the paper says about λ = 2.0** (its runs had not been read when this was registered), and K3's status line says "mixed".
- A value exactly at 0.05 is inside. "Undefined" and "not_pooled" are never rounded.

**Where 0.05 comes from (before any r):** the five reference runs' completion rates lie from 1.5 % below to 0.4 % above their mean (1.656 req/s at λ = 2.0, 1.688 at 2.5; RUN(20261107-083730_sweep_l1pl350_pc_off_r2, load.summarize), RUN(20261107-085650_sweep_l1pl350_pc_off_r2.5, load.summarize); mean 1.681, RUN(the 13 REG-8 runs, gate.reg8_zone)); a fixed cost per turn moves r by under 0.02 (synthetic); the rest is left for drift between days (BELIEF). It is a third of Valse and Jannesari's ±15 %, whose rule is read per run beside ours.

### What is live and what is not, said plainly
Every batch-1 run has been reported. Seen: span completion rates of 1.67 to 1.84 req/s (the [RESULT] entries of REG-11 to REG-18) and first-turn shares of completions of 0.125 to 0.18. From these r can be bounded without computing it: r ≈ (completion rate ÷ 1.681) × (mean served prompt ÷ 3072), about 0.97 to 1.09 with its centre at 1.01 to 1.04 (the review's arithmetic, and ours). So **batch 1's J1 is a measurement whose label is nearly fixed in advance** (inside, or just outside on the high side); a label "low" is out of reach there. The tolerance was chosen knowing those completion rates. **Live:** batch 1's J2 (the two loads' medians), and batch 2's J1, on runs nobody has read. U, the mean served prompt, the busy share and r have not been computed by anyone.

### Prediction (BELIEF)
11–13 runs enter; each is all-miss with probability ≈ 0.9 and busy ≈ 0.9 (REG-18's seeds 3 and 6 each had one quiet span). Median r ≈ 1.02; per run 0.97–1.09. Batch 1: P(J2 inside) ≈ 0.6 (the λ = 2.4 runs reported the highest rates); given J2 inside, P(J1 inside) ≈ 0.8, outside high ≈ 0.2. Slope of r on the load ≈ +0.05 per req/s. Batch 2: P(J1 inside) ≈ 0.7, outside high ≈ 0.25, outside low ≈ 0.05. Valse and Jannesari's token part (±0.15) holds in every judged run ≈ 0.95.

### Decision under each outcome (rule A1)
What does **not** depend on the outcome: the closed-loop margins are stated against E1's floor once it exists (E1 runs in any case), and the confirmation run's low-load phase is sized from the lower of the two capacity readings (U_off ÷ mean served prompt, and the measured cold completion rate).
- **J2 inside and J1 inside:** K3's status says that at λ = 2.0 to 2.4 the stuck engine did the token work of the cache-off sweep (retrospective), with the slope of r on the load beside it; the paper replaces "completes near the cache-off floor" by that sentence and gives the request rate's excess over 1.681 as the served mix. It says nothing about other loads.
- **J2 outside:** the work rate differs between the 2.0 and the 2.4 runs. Load and day coincide in batch 1 (the 2.4 runs are of 2026-11-09, the 2.0 runs of 2026-11-10 and 11), so the wording is "by load or by day". K3 then reports r per group; model F's single capacity is not shown for these loads, and the paper uses model F for no prediction at another load (E0's cost-model prediction is labelled accordingly). This is the outcome that changes K3's claim.
- **J1 outside, high** (pooled, a group, or batch 2): the stuck engine did more token work than the cache-off sweep. Alternatives, in the order they are read: (1) the reference pays for prefill redone after preemptions and the stuck engine does not (the redo readout of both arms); (2) the reference's λ = 2.0 run is low (r against the other four); (3) dev mode; (4) the day. E1 separates none of the first two. K3 reports the excess; the paper's sentence becomes "r times the token work of the cache-off sweep".
- **J1 outside, low:** with the busy precondition this is not idle time. The stuck engine does less work than a miss explains; the comparison of preemptions with matched controls becomes a judged item of C2's condition-4 registration. Out of reach in batch 1 (above); possible in batch 2.
- **Undefined, not_pooled:** reported as such; nothing follows beyond the per-group numbers.

### Reported, never judged (`k3w.row`, `k3w.readouts`, `k3w.reference`)
Per run: its date; U by end time; r against the reference without its λ = 2.0 run; Valse and Jannesari's trap rule (`k3w.vj_rule`: the token part within 0.15, and the session backlog's slope over W above half the excess of the planned session rate over 1.681 / 8); the request rates; the mean served prompt; the mean turn index and first-turn share; sessions completed over started; the median time from first token to end; mean running batch, waiting line and KV; preemptions per second and the redo proxy (preemptions per second × mean prompt ÷ U); Huang and Liu's 5-minute rule; the burst's size; model F's long-run values; model F's replay of the run's schedule at 1.681 without and with the residence. Per load group: n, median, min, max, SD, dates; the least-squares slope of r on the load. A readout that raises is stored as its error message and does not stop the table; the judged values are computed before any readout.

### Alternatives to state with any result
1. **Prefill done twice.** A preempted request is prefilled again; its tokens are counted once. With caching off nothing of the first prefill can be reused; with caching on the freed blocks stay cached until they are overwritten (open-questions S10), which in a full pool is soon. If the two arms redo different shares, r moves by about that difference with no difference in engine speed (synthetic: 8 % redone in the reference only reads r = 1.08). The redo proxy of both arms is the readout.
2. **Load and day coincide in batch 1** (above). The λ = 2.0 group has one run of 2026-11-10 and four of 2026-11-11; their r are listed with dates.
3. **Dev mode.** The burst runs ran with dev mode, the reference sweeps without (PROTOCOL §4; open-questions A6). E1 (cache off, closed loop, no dev mode) and the caching-off control of step D (dev mode) each give a cache-off U in another condition, reported when they exist.
4. **A different saturated state.** The reference is an open-loop sweep whose line grows without bound; the gauges of both arms are reported.
5. **Window edges.** U by first token and by end time, for both arms.
6. **A bimodal set.** A median can sit between two clusters; min, max and SD per group are reported.

### Stopping rule
Each batch is computed once: `uv run python -m analysis.k3w reg21` (batch 1) after this registration is approved; `uv run python -m analysis.k3w reg21 2` after REG-19's [RESULT] (the command raises before).
