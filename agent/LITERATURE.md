## KAIROS (arXiv 2604.16682v2): the open check on Fig. 10–11

**Question (start prompt §2.4):** in Fig. 10–11, did the controller restore full frequency during the thrashing in the run without thrashing avoidance? If yes, that is an accidental removed-trigger observation.

**Answer: the paper does not show it, so it cannot be read either way.**

What the paper says and shows (SUPPORTED):
1. The design has a boost that removes the low frequency when agents fall behind: "If Throughput_k^min falls below the SLO target τ, the controller overrides the context-aware policy and sets the frequency to the maximum level" (§6.2.3, Eq. 11–12, p. 7).
2. It asserts persistence: "even a short overload can push the instance into a persistently inefficient operating region" (§6.2.4, p. 7).
3. The experiment: "It compares three configurations: no frequency control, KAIROS without thrashing avoidance, and KAIROS with thrashing avoidance. While the no-control baseline avoids thrashing by running at high frequency, KAIROS without thrashing avoidance reduces frequency to save power, pushing the system into a thrashing regime." (§7, p. 9). Load 0.05 jobs/s, SLO target 20 tokens/s, mini-swe-agent on DABStep.
4. Fig. 10 (p. 10): per-agent P5 throughput 28.6 (no frequency control), 2.3 (KAIROS without thrashing avoidance), 19.9 tokens/s (with); system job throughput 0.051, 0.046, 0.051 jobs/s.
5. Fig. 11 (p. 10), five panels over 180 minutes: (a) context usage without thrashing avoidance, above the capacity line from about minute 60 to the end; (b) job throughput without it, dipping to about 0.03 jobs/s around minutes 55–75 and then moving between about 0.04 and 0.06; (c) pending agent count, titled "Kairos With Thrashing Avoidance"; (d), (e) context usage and job throughput with it. **No panel shows the GPU frequency of either run.** (Fig. 9, p. 10, shows a frequency trace, with boosts to "Max Freq.", but for a different experiment: 0.03 jobs/s, SLO 35 tokens/s.)
6. The text reads Fig. 11 as: "Without thrashing avoidance, context usage (subfigure (a)) exceeds capacity and remains high, pushing the system into a persistent thrashing regime where throughput drops (subfigure (b)) and queued agents accumulate (subfigure (c)), making recovery difficult." (p. 10). The text gives panel (c) to the run without thrashing avoidance; the panel's own title says "With". An inconsistency inside the paper.

What follows (BELIEF, my inference):
- If the boost of §6.2.3 was active in the configuration "KAIROS without thrashing avoidance", then at a P5 throughput of 2.3 tokens/s against a 20 tokens/s target the controller was at maximum frequency for much of the thrashing, i.e. the cause (low frequency) had been removed while the thrashing went on, at a load that the no-control configuration carries at that same frequency (Fig. 10). That would be a removed-trigger observation, not designed as one.
- The paper states neither whether the boost was active in that configuration nor what the frequency was. So it is not an observation the paper reports.

**Consequence for Related Work.** "Untested with the trigger removed" is too strong as a statement about what happened in their run, and right as a statement about what they report. Wording to use: KAIROS asserts persistence (§6.2.4) and shows thrashing that continues in a run without its concurrency control (Fig. 11a–b); the paper reports no frequency trace for that run, so whether its own boost (§6.2.3) had removed the cause cannot be read from it; no test designed to remove the trigger is reported. (To be applied in `docs/paper/related-work-approach-v3.md`; task Z6a/Z10.)

Other KAIROS facts used by the repo, re-read here (SUPPORTED): engine vLLM (p. 2: "that run vLLM"; the implementation paragraph names "v0.14.0", p. 8, to be re-checked when cited); arrivals of agent jobs are Poisson (p. 3: "using a Poisson distribution"); the motivating thrashing example holds "the request arrival rate … fixed at 0.08 agents/s" while service slows (p. 4), i.e. the cause (lower frequency) stays on.

## Huang and Liu (arXiv 2611.13412v1): read end to end by this session, 2026-11-13

Read: the whole text (`pdftotext -raw`, 1403 lines), abstract to the reference list; Tables 1–4 and the figures were read as text fragments and captions only, not as page images. Everything below is SUPPORTED by the text at the place given unless marked BELIEF.

- **Setup (§3, p. 3).** "vLLM 0.20 (ROCm build 0.20.2rc1.dev253) with automatic prefix caching on single AMD MI300X GPUs, one engine per GPU"; default scheduler "(chunked prefill, FCFS)"; the arms of a pair "share the same base arrival stream … and run on separate GPUs, mostly concurrently"; Qwen3-8B, KV pool fixed at 400K tokens. Sessions: "a 2K-token system prompt shared by all sessions plus a 4K-token task unique to the session, runs 25 turns of 300 output tokens, appends 1K tool tokens per turn, and waits an exponential think time (mean 5 s) between turns; contexts grow from 6K to 37K tokens." Closed loop: "A closed-loop driver issues sessions as a Poisson process of rate λ."
- **Trigger and run (§3).** Extra sessions at "rate 1.5λ for 5 minutes, i.e. 2.5× total arrivals" from minute 15; runs last 90 minutes. Note: their trigger is extra **sessions**, which stay in the system; ours is one-off requests.
- **Their persistence rule (§3).** "A run is persistently degraded if every 5-minute window (non-overlapping, aligned to the end of the trigger) from its first degraded window (hit ratio < 0.5) after the trigger to the end of the run is degraded." (`k3.hl_rule` implements this.)
- **Load (§3).** "At λ=0.035 sessions/s—about 70% of the cache-warm capacity, estimated as ≈0.05 sessions/s".
- **Result (§3, Table 2).** "in 9 of 12 only the burst run degraded persistently, in 0 only the no-burst run did, 2 degraded in both arms and 1 in neither (two-sided sign test over discordant pairs, p = 0.00391)."
- **Spontaneous departures (§3), relevant to K4.** Two of the twelve no-burst twins "degraded spontaneously, 25 and 30 minutes into the run"; "10 of 10 runs at λ=0.04 without a burst stayed healthy for 30 minutes in an earlier sweep". With Qwen3-32B at λ = 0.03 all four no-burst runs degraded after 25–35 minutes, and at 0.025 "a longer no-burst run degraded on its own after about 55 minutes". Their escape-time model "was too pessimistic by an order of magnitude" (§4).
- **Controls (§3).** Caching disabled, "at about 80% of the no-cache engine's capacity", the same absolute burst: the backlog "rises to 17–43 sessions and then falls … two of four runs end at their twins' level …; the other two are still draining". Session cap: "0/10 runs in an earlier matrix". Burst residue: "in 7 of 11 degraded burst runs all injected sessions had finished, yet the hit ratio stayed at 0.10–0.12".
- **Other triggers (§3).** A cache flush with preemption at λ = 0.04 "left 1 of 5 runs persistently degraded". SGLang 0.5.22 absorbed the 2×/2-minute burst "that collapses vLLM in 4 of 10" in 4 of 4 runs; a larger trigger collapsed it in all 6 runs; in the paired design at λ = 0.04 "all 4 SGLang burst runs degraded persistently … while all 4 no-burst twins stayed healthy".
- **The degraded regime (§3, "Anatomy"), relevant to K3 and REG-21.** "99.6–100% of completed turns had less than half of their prompt cached, and the token-weighted hit ratio is 0.10–0.12, which is just the shared system prompt"; "16–32 requests run concurrently, KV usage sits at 83–100%, and preemptions remain rare (23–37 in a 90-minute run)"; "Turn throughput in this regime (0.35–0.46 turns/s) is that of the same engine with prefix caching disabled (0.33–0.43 turns/s". **This is the published form of what REG-21's J1 asks** (their statement is in turns per second; ours is in uncached tokens per second).
- **Drain condition (§4).** S = Σ c(L_k); "The degraded regime can drain only if arrivals bring less work than the engine completes, i.e. if λ < 1/S"; c(L) = −0.06 s + 0.146 ms × L from cache-disabled runs, "S ≈ 78 s and 1/S ≈ 0.013 sessions/s"; fitted on all-miss windows of degraded cache-enabled runs instead, "c(L) = −0.71 s + 0.187 ms × L and 1/S=0.012"; measured break-even "near 0.010–0.014". "This is a necessary condition, not a prediction of the boundary."
- **Recovery (§6, Table 3).** On the seven seeds collapsed in every arm: budgeter 7 of 7 in under 5 minutes, session cap 7 of 7 within 6 minutes, latency AIMD 5 of 7 after 14–25 minutes, admission-only cap 1 of 7, no control 0. Over all collapsed runs of its arm, "The admission-only cap recovered 2 of 8 runs, 21–23 minutes later". The abstract's "throttling new admissions recovered 1 of 7 matched collapsed runs" is the matched count; the introduction's "2 of 8" is the unmatched one. Both are in the paper; cite with the denominator named.
- **Offload (§6).** With vLLM's CPU offloading, "4 of 6 burst runs ended persistently degraded … while all 6 twins stayed healthy"; "Offloading only delays the collapse".
- **On prior systems (§7, Table 4).** KAIROS "argues that even a short overload can push an instance into a persistently inefficient region … but does not test it with a removed trigger or measure recovery once collapsed." Yao et al.: "Their analysis assumes exogenous inter-turn times, excluding the latency feedback that sustains the degraded regime here."
- **Their own limits (§8).** "one GPU type, one replica and finite windows (persistence for 70 minutes after the burst, and 100 minutes after lowering the arrival rate), with no routing, autoscaling, client retries or production evidence."
- **Open-loop statement (§2).** "Unlike open-loop chat traffic, the offered load of a session population depends on the server: a turn cannot be issued until the previous one has been served". No experiment in the text uses open-loop turns (BELIEF as to figures: I searched the text; the figure captions name no such arm).

Against the repo's documents: every statement of `docs/paper/related-work-approach-v3.md` and `paper-plan-v3.md` about this paper that I compared agrees with the text (loads 0.035 and 0.04, 70 %, 9/0/2/1, 80 % with caching off and 2 of 4, 7 of 7 against 2 of 8, vLLM 0.20 on MI300X, separate GPUs, SGLang reproduction, the shared 2K prompt, the escape-time model).
