## Open points that need the owner (or the delegate, with rule F and the owner-proxy)
1. **`harness_fault` leaves n.** It removes a run from n beyond the automatic checks (CLAUDE.md rule C: "marking a run invalid beyond the automatic checks" is the owner's). The rule: the records show more sessions holding a slot than the cap, or sessions waiting at the cap while a slot stood free in more than 5 % of the waiting grid seconds (`d1.harness_fault`; 5 % chosen now, before any capped run). The delegation names "registrations and GPU runs" but not this (FILE(docs/decisions.md:4233)). If it is not approved, the one-line change is `row`'s label: `unclear` in place of `harness_fault` (the run stays in n, neither cut nor stuck).
2. **D1 is stricter than the owner's cap rule in one case.** The owner: "judged on the server's hit rate and waiting line; waiting at the cap is reported apart" (FILE(docs/decisions.md:3949)). D1 does not count a seed as cut when the server is warm but the cap's queue keeps growing (`server_only`), because a cap that only moves a backlog to the client would otherwise pass. `k_cut_server_side` reports the owner's count. Choose before the run: D1's rule (as drafted) or the owner's.
3. **Reuse in place of the "hit rate".** The label reads the prefix reuse of later turns (`c2.reuse_in`), not the hit counter, whose served mix includes first turns and burst requests (decisions.md 2026-11-05 [CORR]; REG-20's readout). The 0.06 thresholds (`recovered_hit_drop`, `stuck_hit_drop`, `hit_climb`) were set on the hit counter (FILE(configs/stage2_base.yaml:17,20), FILE(configs/stage2_cap_base.yaml:9)) and are applied to reuse unchanged. They are fixed before any capped run (rule A5 kept), but they were not calibrated on reuse.
4. **What the cap makes true by construction.** The server's waiting line stays near 0 under any cap of about 23–31, so the label's server side is reuse alone; and a cap below about 34 keeps the pool below 0.80 (first-order bound, BELIEF as a model), so the KV link is cut by construction. D1 then tests whether the cache comes back once the pool is not full. The owner should confirm that this is the test intended, or ask for a different cap rule (that would be a new rule, set before the caps are computed).
5. **`cap_did_not_cut` against claims.md's wording.** claims.md counts "stuck runs that stay stuck under the session cap" as refuting C2 (FILE(docs/claims.md:27)); D1 separates a stuck seed whose pool filled under the cap (`stuck_kv_full`: the cap did not cut the KV link) from one whose pool did not (`stuck_kv_free`: refutes). Choose before the run whether `cap_did_not_cut` is reported as refuting (claims.md) or as not a test (D1's design); the draft gives both readings to the owner.
6. **The decision rule.** "Most" (≥ 3 of 5 cut, no seed stuck) as in REG-18 and REG-19. At n = 5 or 4 with none stuck, the upper bound (0.451, 0.527) lies below REG-19's lower bound 0.549; at n = 3 it does not (0.632). REG-20 uses McNemar's exact test (the owner's choice there); here McNemar's one-sided p is 0.031 only for 5 of 5 cut, so a McNemar rule would need all five. Choose: "most" (drafted) or McNemar.
7. **No fresh uncapped run.** The capped runs run days after REG-19's, on a later harness commit; the only drift check inside D1 is `gate.reg19_baseline_pair` before the burst. REG-20's drift rerun (`*_burst_l1pl350cl_r2.0_w3000_s15_drift`) is sealed and not used. Option: add one uncapped rerun of seed 15 (≈ 1.25 GPU h, labelled single seed), as a readout. A design addition, not drafted.

## What D2 (caching off, matched on utilization) still needs before it can be drafted
1. **A closed-loop cache-off capacity.** The plan matches D2's load to "the same share of the caching-off capacity as the test load has of the warm capacity" and says which capacity must be fixed in the registration (FILE(docs/experiment-plan.md:424)): the keep-up μ_off = 1.0 req/s (open loop, Stage 1) or the saturation floor 1.681 (open loop, REG-8). Both are open-loop numbers. E1 measures the closed-loop cache-off floor and is not yet run; its floor is withheld until E0's [REG] (E1 draft 7). D2 needs E1's [RESULT], or a decision to use an open-loop number with that confound named.
2. **The matched load.** With the share 2.0 ÷ 2.4 ≈ 0.83 of the closed-loop warm capacity (FILE(analysis/k2.py:26)): ≈ 0.83 × μ_off. With μ_off = 1.0 that is ≈ 0.83 req/s; with a closed-loop keep-up capacity from E1 it is 0.83 × that.
3. **The same absolute burst is a much larger shock** at a load of about 0.83 req/s than at 2.0: 2.4 req/s of 2944-token prompts for 180 s against a server completing about 1 req/s. The registration must say whether the burst stays absolute (the plan) and name this.
4. **New schedules and a new control.** The rate changes the schedule (`build_schedule` reads `workload.rate`), so D2 has no REG-19 control: it needs its own no-burst control per seed, or a stated reason to skip it.
5. **A verdict without hit.** With caching off, hit and reuse are 0 throughout; "recovered" must be read on the waiting line and completions only ("one level only, and the backlog drains", the plan's expectation). REG-19's whole-window test may fit D2 better than D1 (no cap), but its hit-based clauses do not.
6. **Seals.** D2's run names must avoid E1's `*_sweep_l1pl350cl_pc_off_r*` and E0's `*_sweep_l1t16pl350cl_pc_o*_r*` patterns, and E1's floor must not be read before E0's [REG] if D2 uses it.
7. **Its own rule F review and its cost** (≈ 2 × 5 × 1.25 h if each seed needs a control and a burst run: ≈ 12.5 GPU h, DERIVED, or half that without controls).

## Assumptions made (each BELIEF unless tagged)
1. Each seed's cap lies in 23–31 (median ≈ 26), from a rough M/G/∞ argument (sessions at 0.25/s, ≈ 55–58 s per healthy session, REG-19's arrival surges). Checked by the registered caps computation, before any run.
2. A cold server's completion rate does not rise under the cap (it stays ≈ 1.75 req/s, below the load), so a cap queue back at its baseline means the server left the cold state. Readout: `spans`' `completion_rate`.
3. KV per running request in a capped cold state equals the uncapped cold state's (0.951 ÷ 30; REG-22 batch 2, FILE(docs/decisions.md:16752-16756)), and ≈ 8.75 sessions sit in think time (1.75 req/s × 5 s). These give the first-order bound (cap < 33.99 keeps KV < 0.80). Consistency check (DERIVED): 0.951 ÷ 30 × 96,656 tokens (FILE(configs/l1_base.yaml:16)) ≈ 3,064 tokens per running request, near the 2,931–3,067 mean served prompt of the cold state reported by REG-21 batch 1 (`docs/status.md`).
4. vLLM 0.32.0 keeps a freed block's prefix until the block is reallocated, reuses free blocks least recently used first, and its KV usage gauge (`vllm:kv_cache_usage_perc`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:38)) counts only blocks of running requests. Not checked against the pinned source (vLLM is not installed on the Mac); for `docs/open-questions.md`: "SOURCE(vLLM 0.32.0 vllm/v1/core/block_pool.py and the KV usage metric): are cached free blocks counted in `vllm:kv_cache_usage_perc`, and in what order are free blocks reused?" The prediction's prefix-survival arithmetic depends on it; the label does not.
5. P(valid) ≈ 0.8 per attempt, from REG-19's 10 counted of 12 launches (two lost to power-offs, FILE(docs/decisions.md:16560)).
6. The per-seed label probabilities and the independence of seeds in the enumeration (the prediction only).
7. The capped run's pre-burst trajectory equals the control's up to latency jitter (same schedule before 900 s, FILE(harness/workload.py:24-26); `d1.inputs`).
8. The fake-server tests of the cap (FILE(tests/test_session_cap.py)) carry over to the GPU; D1 is the first GPU use.
9. The drafted cost uses REG-19's start-up time (108 s, FILE(docs/decisions.md:3819)) and the drain cap of 300 s (FILE(configs/base.yaml:43)).
10. The REG-19 counted runs are those of FILE(docs/decisions.md:16533-16537), and their seal has lifted (FILE(docs/decisions.md:16567)); D1 reads REG-19's controls (for the caps) and burst runs (for the uncapped spans) only after D1's approval.
