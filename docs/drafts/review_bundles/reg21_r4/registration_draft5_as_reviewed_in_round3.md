### Statistic (`analysis/k3w.py`)
- **U** (`work_rate`, FILE(analysis/k3w.py:101)): uncached prompt tokens per second, Σ (prompt_tokens − cached_tokens) over ok requests, session and burst, whose first token falls in the window (the first token ends a request's prefill). It raises if an ok request has no first-token time or no prompt length. U is a mean over the whole window; its lowest span is not reported.
- **U_off** (`reference`, FILE(analysis/k3w.py:128)): the mean of U over REG-8's five saturated cache-off runs, pinned by id (FILE(analysis/k3w.py:71): `sweep_l1pl350_pc_off` at λ = 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, the runs that give the floor 1.681 req/s, RUN(the 13 REG-8 runs, gate.reg8_zone)), each over its measured window. It raises unless exactly those five are there and valid. All five count, and they are not held to the busy precondition; r against the four runs at λ ≥ 2.5 is reported beside r.
- **r** = U over W ÷ U_off, per run. W = [burst end + 600 s, burst end + watch): [1680, 2580) s; [1680, 5580) s for REG-15; [1680, 4080) s for REG-19's runs (DERIVED from the configs: burst end 1080 s, watch 1500, 4500 and 3000 s).
- **Why r:** it contains no turn mix, no residence, no queue discipline and no think time. On synthetic servers (FILE(tests/test_k3w.py:156) onward; no run read): an all-miss first-come-first-served server reads r = 1.00 ± 0.02 at λ = 2.0 and 2.4; so does a running batch of 16 or of 64 slots and a six-fold think time; a fixed cost per turn moves r by under 0.02; a trap at 1.85 or 1.55 req/s reads 1.10 or 0.92; a work rate that differs by load fails J2.
- **Model F's own number** (`docs/model.md` M5): the trapped server works at the all-miss capacity whatever the load, so r = 1.00 and a spread of 0 between loads; with a fixed cost per turn, within 0.02 of that.
- **What r cannot see:** hits below the precondition (r subtracts cached tokens; the precondition, not J1, answers whether the state is all-miss), and prefill done twice after a preemption (each request's tokens are counted once). A run that redoes prefill is credited with less work than it did.

### Runs and preconditions
- **Batch 1** (`k3w.batch1_runs`, FILE(analysis/k3w.py:55)): the 13 closed-loop L1 burst runs at 350 W made before REG-19: REG-11 (λ = 2.4, seed 1), REG-12 (2.4, seeds 2–6), REG-14 (2.2, seed 2), REG-15 (2.2, seed 12), REG-17 (2.0, seed 2), REG-18 (2.0, seeds 3–6). **The function raises unless all 13 are there and valid.** No other run of this kind exists: the configs `burst_l1pl350cl_r2.0_s7` (REG-18's unused fifth seed) and `confirm_l1pl350cl_r2.2_long_s14` have no run folder (`ls runs`, names only).
- **The groups share seeds (rule H).** Seeds 2–6 were run at both λ = 2.0 and 2.4, and seed 2 also at 2.2. The schedules at two loads are not the same draws, so this is stated, not used.
- **Batch 2** (`k3w.batch2_runs`, FILE(analysis/k3w.py:338)): REG-19's burst runs, one valid run per seed, the seeds a prefix of 15–19 and at least three of them. The function raises before `docs/decisions.md` has a heading that is REG-19's result (`## <date> · [RESULT] REG-19…`; a heading that only quotes those words lifts nothing, FILE(analysis/withheld.py:39)), on two valid runs of a seed, on a gap, and on fewer than three seeds.
- **A run enters** when `k1.cold_backlogged` holds in its own end window. **It is judged** only if, in W, it is
  - (a) **all-miss:** prefix reuse of later turns (`k1.reuse`) ≤ 0.05; and
  - (b) **busy:** at least one request waiting in at least 0.99 of the scrapes of W (`k3w.busy_share`, FILE(analysis/k3w.py:114)), **and the gauge covers W**: a scrape within 10 s of each end and no gap above 10 s (`load._covered`). A missing gauge is listed as "no waiting gauge in W", not as idleness.
- **What the gauge counts:** `vllm:num_requests_waiting` is the scheduler's waiting queues without the deferred requests; a request in the running set is not counted (SOURCE(vLLM 0.32.0 vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py:Scheduler.make_stats), SOURCE(vLLM 0.32.0 vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py:Scheduler.get_request_counts)). So an engine that holds every request in its running set reads 0 and is left out, though it may be working at capacity. In the published cold spans the running set was about 30 with KV at 0.95 and 54 or more requests waiting (RUN(20261110-182920_burst_l1pl350cl_r2.0_s2, gate.reg17_gate)), so this is not expected to bite (BELIEF).
- **Why busy:** an engine whose line empties in part of W does less work for a reason that is not its capacity; the draft-3 review's synthetic all-miss server with a lull reads r = 0.69 to 0.91 (`rulef-reg21-v3-2.md`, a2).
- **Runs left out are listed with every reason that applies:** "no later turn in W", "not all-miss in W", "no waiting gauge in W", "idle in W", "no work rate". Their r is listed beside them.

### What was known when the thresholds were fixed
Every batch-1 run has been reported, and both tolerances were fixed knowing these numbers. U, the mean served prompt, `k1.reuse`, the busy share and r have not been computed on any run by anyone.
- **Completion rates over W, per run** (W is the three published 300 s spans): at λ = 2.0, 1.753 (RUN(20261110-182920_burst_l1pl350cl_r2.0_s2, gate.reg17_gate)) and 1.769, 1.748, 1.720, 1.761 for seeds 4, 3, 5, 6 (RUN(REG-18's four burst runs, gate.reg18_table); each is the mean of its three span rates, DERIVED). At λ = 2.4, 1.79 to 1.83 over the backlogged stretch (RUN(REG-12's five burst runs, gate.reg12_table)).
- **What that implies for r** (DERIVED): r ≈ (completion rate ÷ 1.681) × (mean served prompt ÷ 3072), where 3072 tokens is the mean prompt of the eight turns (DERIVED: 2176 + 256 × 3.5). The first factor is 1.02 to 1.05 at λ = 2.0 (median 1.04) and 1.06 to 1.09 at λ = 2.4. The second is 0.98 to 1.00 if first turns are 0.125 to 0.18 of the completions and the other turns are even (DERIVED: mean prompt = 3200 − 1024 × first-turn share; REG-17's share was 0.141 to 0.146, same RUN tag).
- **So, before anything is computed:** the λ = 2.0 group's median is near 1.03 and the λ = 2.4 group's near 1.05 to 1.08. **J2's spread is expected at 0.02 to 0.05, at its tolerance; the pooled J1 at 1.04 to 1.06, at its tolerance on the high side; a label "low" is out of reach in batch 1.** Batch 1's labels are therefore nearly fixed in advance and say little. The one informative outcome there is "outside".
- **The preconditions were also fixed knowing things.** Reuse ≤ 0.05 dates from draft 2 and has no derivation; every published span of these runs has a counter hit share of 0.000 to 0.003, so it is expected to pass. Busy ≥ 0.99 comes from the draft-3 review, and was fixed knowing that REG-18's seeds 3 and 6 each had one span planned below the cold rate, with the line still at 94 to 154 on average in those spans (RUN(REG-18's burst runs, gate.reg18_table)). If those two runs were left out all the same, the λ = 2.0 group would have exactly three runs.
- **The only item on unread data is batch 2's J1.** For it to be read as such, this [REG] and its delegate [DECISION] are committed before REG-19's table is computed or read by anyone; the entry says so. If that is not true when batch 2 is computed, batch 2 is labelled retrospective and does not govern.

### Alternatives to state with any result
1. **Prefill done twice.** A preempted request is prefilled again; its tokens are counted once. With caching off nothing of the first prefill can be reused; with caching on the freed blocks stay cached until they are overwritten (open-questions S10), which in a full pool is soon. If the two arms redo different shares, r moves by about that difference with no difference in engine speed (synthetic: 8 % redone in the reference only reads r = 1.08, `rulef-reg21-v3-2.md`, a1). The redo proxy of both arms is the readout.
2. **Load and day coincide in batch 1** (above). The λ = 2.0 group has one run of 2026-11-10 and four of 2026-11-11; their r are listed with dates. The fixed-load contrast is the one reading of the same load on other days.
3. **Dev mode.** The burst runs ran with dev mode, the reference sweeps without (PROTOCOL §4; open-questions A6). E1 (cache off, closed loop, no dev mode) and the caching-off control of step D (dev mode) each give a cache-off U in another condition, reported when they exist.
4. **A different saturated state.** The reference is an open-loop sweep whose line grows without bound; the gauges of both arms are reported.
5. **Window edges.** U by first token and by end time, for both arms.
6. **A bimodal set.** A median can sit between two clusters; min, max and SD per group are reported.
