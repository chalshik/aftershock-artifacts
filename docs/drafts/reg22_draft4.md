## 2026-11-13 · [REG] REG-22 (C2, condition 4): in the stuck burst runs, is the pool full and are the loop's links present in every span after the burst's own requests have left? A description of the 13 burst runs made before REG-19, most of it already computed by their gates, and the same table once on REG-19's burst runs; the plan's trace order is not judged (0 GPU h)

- **ID:** REG-22. An analysis of existing runs, 0 GPU h. Draft 4. Code: `analysis/c2.py` and `tests/test_c2.py` at commit `727d1f2`. Drafts 1 to 3 are in `docs/drafts/`. Review: rule F on draft 3 (FILE(docs/drafts/reviews-2026-11-13/rulef-reg22-draft3-r1.md), NOT READY, ten items); what changed because of it is listed at the end.
- **What this is, in one line:** batch 1 is a description of runs that have all been reported, and most of its values were already computed by registered gates; batch 2 is the same table once on runs whose outcomes nobody has read. Neither is called a test below.
- **What it can and cannot show, said first (rule A1).**
  - The label `loop` says that the pool is full and the cache is lost in every 300 s span after the burst left. **It does not say why.** A queue that the load alone sustains gets the same label: a queue fills a pool that holds about 30 requests, and a full pool evicts prefixes (DERIVED: 96,656 tokens of pool ÷ (3072 + 128) tokens per request at the mean prompt = 30.2; the review's computation from the configs). What separates a loop from an overload is conditions 2, 5 and 6 and the loop-cut controls, not this table; the same-seed control's values are reported beside each run for that reason.
  - The four links are about two independent facts: the reuse of later turns and KV. The running batch follows from KV, and the uncached rate follows from the reuse unless throughput falls below about half of the offered rate (the review's computation, same file).
  - So the outcomes that carry information are `not_sustained` and `undefined`. `not_memory_limited` would change a claim but is close to excluded for batch 1 by what is already published.
- **This registration covers computing and reporting only.** Every edit to `docs/claims.md`, `docs/paper/` or PROTOCOL, and the registration of D1 and D2, needs its own decision with its own reviews. The lines under "Decision under each outcome" are a ceiling on what may be said.
- **Question:** in the burst runs that the registered verdict calls `stuck`, is the KV pool full, is the running batch at least twice its baseline and below the 256 cap (the registered levels), and do the loop's links (cache lost, prompts recomputed, large batch, full pool) hold together in every 300 s span once no burst request is left in the system?
- **Claim:** C2 (`docs/claims.md`), its first test: "in stuck runs … condition 4 (mean KV ≥ 0.80, mean N ≥ 2 × baseline, N below the 256 cap)". PROTOCOL §3 condition 4 adds "and the traces follow the plan's order".
- **The reading of the order clause, and whose it is.** The plan's order (the hit rate drops, recomputed prefill rises, the batch grows, KV fills, the hit rate stays low) describes a cache wipe, where the loss comes first. BELIEF, untested: on a burst run the first events are the trigger's own (batch and KV up), and the hit rate falls after them; nobody has computed the onsets. Judging the literal order would then fail every burst run for a reason that says nothing about the loop. So for burst runs the clause is read as **`sustain`** below, and the onset times are reported. `sustain` is stronger than the end-window levels in duration and weaker than an order test in kind: it tests that the links are present together in every span, and no direction. **This is a change to how a PROTOCOL rule is read, which CLAUDE.md C reserves for the owner; here it is the delegate's, and it is listed for the owner.** If this registration is approved, PROTOCOL §3 gets a dated note pointing here, as its own entry. Wherever a result says "condition 4", it says "as read by REG-22, the plan's order not judged".

### Statistic (`analysis/c2.py`)
Baseline: the 600 s before the burst starts ([300, 900) s in all 18 configs). End window: the last 600 s of the watch. Spans: the watch cut into 300 s spans from the burst's end (1080 s); watches are 1500 s (eleven runs), 4500 s (REG-15) and 3000 s (REG-19's), all multiples of 300 (the review's check of the configs).
- **levels** (`c2.levels`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:85); the thresholds of decisions.md 2026-11-01): in the end window, mean KV ≥ 0.80, mean running batch N ≥ 2 × its baseline mean, and maximum N < 256. The function names which of the three failed. In this setup the cap cannot be reached (the pool holds at most 42 requests at the smallest prompt), and N ≥ 2 × baseline can fail only if the baseline N is above 15.
- **A gauge value needs coverage** (`c2.gauge`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:48)): scrapes within 10 s of each end of its window and no gap above 10 s (`load._covered`); otherwise the value is missing.
- **The exit** (`c2.last_burst_end`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:101)): the moment the last burst request ended. It is missing if a burst request never ended, ended with an error status, or the run has no burst rows.
- **links** (`c2.links`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:111)), over one span:
  1. reuse of later turns (cached tokens over the previous prompt, by first token; at least 20 later turns, else missing) ≤ baseline − 0.06 (the verdict's `stuck_hit_drop`, 0.06 in every config);
  2. session uncached prefill (prompt tokens not served from the cache, session requests only, per second, by first token) ≥ 2 × baseline;
  3. mean N ≥ 2 × baseline;
  4. mean KV ≥ 0.80.
  The function names the links that fail. The uncached rate raises if an ok session request has no first-token time or prompt length; a request without `cached_tokens` counts as a full miss, and a request with a first token that never finished is not counted (both are counted and reported, `c2.row_counts`).
- **sustain** (`c2.sustain`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:125)): a span is judged when it starts at or after the exit. Every span of the watch is reported with that flag, so the stretch between the exit and the first judged span (up to 299 s) can be seen though it is not judged.
- **What a link's threshold lets through.** A span is 300 s. A full recovery of up to about 70 s inside one span does not fail any link, and the reuse link (a drop of 0.06 from a baseline near 0.84) still holds with the cache back for most of a span; a re-warming span shows first in the uncached rate (the review's trace E; FILE(tests/test_c2.py), `test_a_span_in_which_the_cache_comes_back_breaks_sustain`: the cache back for 250 of 300 s gives reuse 0.83 and fails only link 2). So `loop` means "no span re-warmed for most of its length", at 300 s resolution.
- **Why 2 × for the uncached rate (fixed before any run is read for it):** DERIVED from the L1 schedule. Warm, a session of 8 turns prefills 2176 + 7 × 256 = 3968 tokens; cold, 24,576. At equal request rates the cold rate is 6.2 × the warm one (DERIVED: 24,576 / 3968); at the cold completion rates on record (1.73 to 1.81 req/s at λ = 2.0 and 2.2, RUN(20261110-182920_burst_l1pl350cl_r2.0_s2, gate.reg17_gate), RUN(20261110-063647_burst_l1pl350cl_r2.2_s2, gate.reg14_gate)) against 2.0 to 2.4 req/s offered it is about 4.5 to 5.6 × (DERIVED). The link fails when the share of turns that hit reaches 0.81 at equal request rates (DERIVED: 24,576 − 20,608 h = 2 × 3968), and already at 0.66 to 0.77 at those cold completion rates (the review's computation).

### Labels (`c2.condition4`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:172))
In this order:
1. The levels cannot be read (a gauge does not cover the end window or the baseline): `undefined`.
2. The levels fail: `not_memory_limited`, whatever `sustain` is.
3. The levels hold and there are fewer than two judged spans (or no exit): `undefined`.
4. The levels hold and a judged span fails a link: `not_sustained`, even if another judged span has a missing value.
5. The levels hold, no judged span fails, and one has a missing value: `undefined`.
6. Otherwise: `loop`.

Reported per run with the level or links that failed, and as counts. Per load: k of n `loop` with its one-sided 95 % Clopper–Pearson lower and upper bounds (`stats.cp_upper`, rule H), never pooled across loads. **A sentence about a load needs at least 3 stuck runs at it;** below that the runs are reported one by one. λ = 2.2 has two runs with watches of 1500 s and 4500 s (4 and 14 spans): they are never counted together. Seeds 2–6 were run at both λ = 2.0 and 2.4 and seed 2 also at 2.2, and the schedules of one seed at two loads are nested (FILE(tests/test_k3w.py:438)), so the loads' counts are paired by seed and are not independent samples.

### Decision under each outcome (rule A1)
Each line is tied to the component that failed, and to at least 3 stuck runs at a load for any sentence about that load.
- **`loop` in more than half of the stuck runs at a load (n ≥ 3):** it may be said that, as read by REG-22 with the plan's order not judged, the registered levels held and the links were present in every 300 s span after the burst left, in k of n stuck runs at that load, with both bounds; C2's status may then read "condition 4, as read by REG-22, met in k of n stuck runs, described retrospectively; an overload gives the same picture; the loop-cut controls not yet run". D1 and D2 are proposed on the session population as planned.
- **`not_memory_limited`:** worded by the level that failed. Only if KV < 0.80 may it be said that the pool was not full; then the stuck runs at that load are backlog states and that is against C2 for those cells, D1 and D2 are not proposed as planned, and the paper would say so. If only N < 2 × baseline failed with KV ≥ 0.80, the pool was full and the wording is "the running batch was less than twice its baseline", with the baseline given. Not expected (above).
- **`not_sustained`:** worded by the link that failed in the span that failed. "Re-warmed" only if the reuse link failed; if only the uncached rate failed, "the recomputed work fell below twice its baseline in k spans", with the span's reuse and completion rate beside it (a partly re-warmed span and a dip in throughput both give this). If it is the label of more than half of the stuck runs at a load (n ≥ 3), the confirmation run's registration must read every span, not only the end window.
- **Mixed, `undefined`, or fewer than 3 stuck runs at a load:** reported per run; C2's status for that load does not change.

### Reported, never judged
- **Per run:** the baseline; the levels with what failed; every span of the watch with its four values, the links that failed and whether it was judged; the exit; the row counts (`c2.row_counts`).
- **The same-seed control** (`c2.control_readout`, FILE(analysis/c2.py:284)): its registered verdict and its links over the burst run's judged spans, against its own baseline. A queue that the load alone explains would show the links in the control too. REG-15's run has no control. This is the cheap separator the review asked for; it carries no label.
- **Onsets** (`c2.onsets`): the first 10 s grid time after the burst's start at which the trailing 60 s crosses: KV ≥ 0.9; N ≥ 2 × baseline; reuse ≤ baseline − 0.06 with at least 20 turns; session uncached rate ≥ 2 × baseline. Equal times are reported as one group. **The crossing lags differ by construction:** for a step at one instant, a trailing 60 s mean crosses after 60 × (level − before) ÷ (after − before) seconds, which is about 4 s for the reuse (DERIVED: 60 × 0.06 / 0.84), 12 to 14 s for the uncached rate (60 × 1 / 4.4 to 5.2), 15 s for N (60 × 6 / 24) and 56 s for KV (60 × 0.7 / 0.75). So signals that move at the same instant are reported in the plan's order, reuse first and KV last. **An order found is said to differ from simultaneity only where two onsets are further apart than the difference of their lags; if the order found is the plan's within those lags, the result says "not distinguishable from simultaneous".**
- **The eviction link read directly** (`c2.eviction_readout`): the engine samples 1 % of its KV blocks and, when a sampled block is taken for a new allocation, records the time since its last access (`vllm:kv_block_idle_before_evict_seconds`; SOURCE(vLLM 0.32.0 vllm/v1/core/kv_cache_metrics.py:KVCacheMetricsCollector.on_block_evicted); SOURCE(vLLM 0.32.0 vllm/v1/core/block_pool.py:_maybe_evict_cached_block)). For the baseline and for the stretch after the exit: what the histogram gained, the bucket holding the median, the shares at or below 5 s and 30 s, and the median time between the first tokens of a session's consecutive turns (`c2.turn_spacing`). A counter that goes down inside the window is flagged (`reset`), not read. The histogram has bucket resolution: where the turn spacing falls inside the median's bucket, the two are reported as not separable.
  - **Prediction (DERIVED in `agent/theory/k1_held_prefix.md`; BELIEF as a forecast):** warm, an idle prefix is overwritten about 64 s after its release, so the median falls in the (60, 120] s bucket, far above a turn spacing of about 7 s; in the cold state about 1 s after its release, so the median is the time a request holds its blocks plus that, in the (10, 20] or (20, 30] s bucket, and below the turn spacing there.
  - **If it is wrong:** a cold-state median above the turn spacing would mean prefixes outlive the gap and are still not reused, which the loop does not explain; a warm median below the turn spacing would contradict the warm hit rate of 0.84. Either is reported as against the loop's eviction link.

### Alternatives to state with any result
1. **Overload, not a loop.** At λ = 2.4 the closed-loop warm capacity is reached (FILE(docs/claims.md:45)), and one of REG-12's controls fell into the same cold, full, backlogged state with no burst (RUN(REG-12's five controls, gate.reg12_table)). There the label `loop` holds with no history dependence. The control readout shows it per run; conditions 2, 5 and 6 decide it.
2. **The label follows from the selection.** The verdict `stuck` already requires a low hit rate at the end; the levels then nearly follow. What is not selected on is the spans before the end window.
3. **The preemption cycle of Ao et al.** A full pool with a large batch is also what their limit cycle shows. Preemptions per second against the same seed's control are the registered comparison (decisions.md 2026-11-01); it is computed in its own registration.
4. **A schedule lull.** A span planned below the cold rate can shrink the batch and KV with the cache still lost; the planned session rate of each span is in the published tables, and `not_sustained` is worded by the link that failed.
5. **More spans, more chances.** A run with 14 judged spans is likelier to be `not_sustained` than one with 4; the number of judged spans is reported per run and no rate is compared across watch lengths.
6. **The deepest backlogs drop out.** The longest burst tails leave the fewest judged spans, so the most stuck runs are the likeliest to be `undefined`; the spans that exist are reported for those runs.
